How this income tax calculator works

Enter your yearly income and, if you want to compare, the deductions you claim. The calculator works out your tax under both the new and the old tax regime at the same time, applies the standard deduction, the Section 87A rebate (with marginal relief), surcharge and the 4% health & education cess, and highlights the regime that costs you less. Everything runs in your browser — nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere.

The slabs are the ones introduced in Union Budget 2025, which continue unchanged for FY 2026-27 (tax year 2026-27, the first year under the Income-tax Act, 2025) and also apply to FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), so you can use it both for planning this year and for filing your return.

  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 for salaried employees and pensioners.
  • Rebate (Section 87A): up to ₹60,000, so there is no tax if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh. For salaried people that means zero tax on a salary up to ₹12.75 lakh.
  • Marginal relief: if your taxable income is just above ₹12 lakh, your tax cannot be more than the income above ₹12 lakh. Example: on ₹12.10 lakh the slab tax is ₹61,500, but you pay only ₹10,000 + cess.
  • The new regime is the default. Most deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest on a self-occupied house) are not allowed; employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2) is.

Under the old regime the standard deduction is ₹50,000 and the 87A rebate is ₹12,500, so there is no tax up to ₹5 lakh of taxable income. In exchange you can claim deductions such as 80C (₹1.5 lakh), 80D, HRA, home-loan interest (₹2 lakh), NPS 80CCD(1B) (₹50,000) and more.

Surcharge and cess

A surcharge applies on high incomes: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and in the old regime 37% above ₹5 crore (the new regime caps it at 25%). Marginal relief on surcharge is applied automatically. A 4% health and education cess is then added on tax plus surcharge.

Worked example: ₹15 lakh salary

New regime: ₹15,00,000 − ₹75,000 standard deduction = ₹14,25,000 taxable. Tax = ₹20,000 (4–8L) + ₹40,000 (8–12L) + ₹33,750 (12–14.25L at 15%) = ₹93,750, plus 4% cess = ₹97,500.

Old regime with ₹1.5 lakh 80C and ₹25,000 80D: ₹15,00,000 − ₹50,000 − ₹1,75,000 = ₹12,75,000 taxable. Tax = ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹82,500 = ₹1,95,000, plus cess = ₹2,02,800. The new regime saves over ₹1 lakh here. The old regime usually wins only when you have large HRA and home-loan deductions.

Old vs new regime: which should you choose?

  • Break-even deductions (beyond the standard deduction) for a salaried person below 60: about ₹5.4 lakh at ₹15 lakh salary and ₹7.1 lakh at ₹20 lakh. If your deductions are lower than this, the new regime is cheaper. Up to ₹12.75 lakh salary the new regime is always better because the tax is zero.
  • If you pay high rent in a metro and also have a home loan, check both — the calculator shows the exact break-even for your numbers.
  • Salaried employees can switch regime every year when filing the return. Those with business income can switch back to the old regime only once.

Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, under the new regime. The Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 makes the tax zero when taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh. Salaried people also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, so a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax. The rebate does not cover special-rate income such as capital gains.

How much tax do I pay on a ₹15 lakh salary?

Under the new regime about ₹97,500 including cess (taxable income ₹14.25 lakh after the ₹75,000 standard deduction). Under the old regime it depends on your deductions; with only 80C and 80D it is about ₹2.03 lakh.

Which is better, the old or the new tax regime?

For most people earning up to ₹25 lakh with modest deductions, the new regime is cheaper. The old regime can win if you claim large HRA, home-loan interest and 80C/80D deductions together. Use the calculator above to compare your exact numbers.

What is marginal relief under the new regime?

If your taxable income is slightly above ₹12 lakh, the tax you pay cannot exceed the amount by which your income exceeds ₹12 lakh. For example, on ₹12.10 lakh you pay ₹10,000 plus cess instead of ₹61,500.

Is the standard deduction available in both regimes?

Yes. Salaried employees and pensioners get ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old regime.

Did Budget 2026 change the income tax slabs?

No. The slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the ₹12 lakh rebate limit introduced in Budget 2025 continue for FY 2026-27. The new Income-tax Act, 2025 renumbers sections but keeps these amounts.
